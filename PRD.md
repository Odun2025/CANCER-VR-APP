**Product Requirements Document (PRD)**

## **CancerCompass VR — Immersive Cancer Support & Wellbeing Experience**

**Product type:** Virtual reality wellbeing and cancer-support application  
**Primary users:** Cancer patients  
**Secondary users:** Caregivers and family members  
**Product owner/brand:** CancerCompass And Med Crescent Pharmacy  
**Core purpose:** To help people affected by cancer manage anxiety, treatment-related distress, discomfort, information needs and emotional wellbeing through immersive, supportive experiences.

---

## **1\. Product Vision**

Cancer treatment can be physically and emotionally overwhelming. Patients may experience fear before treatment, discomfort during treatment, uncertainty about their medicines and diagnosis, and emotional exhaustion between hospital visits.

**CancerCompass VR** will provide a safe, compassionate digital space where patients can choose experiences according to what they need at that moment.

The central philosophy is:

**“What do you need right now?”**

Instead of presenting patients with a complicated collection of features, the experience should guide them toward the type of support they need while allowing them to remain in control.

The product should complement—not replace—professional cancer care.

---

# **2\. Problem Statement**

Cancer patients and caregivers may face:

* Anxiety before chemotherapy and medical procedures  
* Fear associated with diagnosis and treatment  
* Treatment-related discomfort and boredom  
* Difficulty relaxing during lengthy treatment sessions  
* Information overload or difficulty understanding treatment  
* Emotional isolation  
* Uncertainty about what to expect  
* Caregiver stress  
* Difficulty finding trustworthy, understandable health information  
* A lack of culturally familiar wellbeing experiences

The product addresses these needs through **immersive distraction, relaxation, education, emotional support and connection.**

---

# **3\. Product Goals**

### **Primary goals**

1. Reduce feelings of anxiety and treatment-related distress.  
2. Provide meaningful distraction during treatment.  
3. Promote relaxation and emotional wellbeing.  
4. Help patients understand aspects of their cancer journey.  
5. Give patients greater control over how they experience treatment.  
6. Provide appropriate supportive experiences at home and between hospital visits.  
7. Give caregivers useful resources for supporting their loved ones.  
8. Create culturally relevant experiences, including African and Nigerian environments.

### **Secondary goals**

* Encourage positive engagement with treatment.  
* Improve patients' sense of preparedness.  
* Create opportunities for safe emotional expression.  
* Strengthen the relationship between patients, caregivers and trusted health information.  
* Generate useful, ethically collected insights about patients' support needs.

---

# **4\. Target Users**

## **Primary: Cancer patients**

The initial product should accommodate adults receiving different forms of cancer treatment, including chemotherapy, while remaining flexible enough to support patients at different stages of their cancer journey.

### **Patient needs**

A patient may want to:

* Escape temporarily  
* Relax  
* Understand  
* Feel encouraged  
* Distract themselves  
* Prepare for treatment  
* Feel less alone  
* Express how they are feeling  
* Find something comforting while receiving treatment

---

## **Secondary: Caregivers and family**

Caregivers should have a dedicated experience rather than simply being given access to the patient's interface.

They may need:

* Simple explanations of what the patient may be experiencing  
* Guidance on providing emotional support  
* Encouragement and wellbeing resources  
* Educational content  
* Ways to participate in appropriate shared experiences

---

# **5\. Core Product Concept**

The experience revolves around five pillars:

### **🌿 ESCAPE**

Take the patient's mind somewhere peaceful.

### **💙 SUPPORT**

Help the patient manage emotions and feel encouraged.

### **🔬 UNDERSTAND**

Make cancer and treatment information easier to understand.

### **🎧 DISTRACT**

Provide engaging experiences during treatment.

### **🤝 CONNECT**

Help patients feel connected to loved ones and their support network.

These pillars should work together rather than feel like separate products.

---

# **6\. Core Patient Journey**

The recommended patient journey is:

**Open app → Check in → Identify need → Receive recommendation → Choose experience → Immersive experience → Check in afterward → Continue journey**

### **Example**

A patient opens the app and sees:

**“How are you feeling today?”**

They select:

**Anxious**

The app responds:

**“I'm here with you. What would help right now?”**

Options:

* Help me relax  
* Take my mind somewhere else  
* Help me understand  
* Encourage me  
* Talk to someone  
* I'm not sure

The app then recommends an appropriate experience while preserving patient choice.

---

# **7\. Feature Requirements**

## **Feature 1: Emotional Check-In**

Patients should be able to indicate how they feel.

Possible emotions include:

* Anxious  
* Afraid  
* Sad  
* Tired  
* Lonely  
* Uncomfortable  
* Calm  
* Hopeful  
* Confused  
* Overwhelmed  
* Happy

The patient should also have an **“I'm not sure”** option.

### **Requirement**

The app should never make the patient feel that they have selected the “wrong” emotion.

---

# **8\. Feature 2: “What Do You Need Right Now?”**

This becomes the **signature feature** of CancerCompass VR.

Patients can choose:

**🌿 Take me somewhere peaceful**  
**🧘 Help me relax**  
**🎧 Distract me**  
**🔬 Help me understand**  
**💙 Encourage me**  
**🤝 Help me connect**

The app may recommend an experience based on the patient's check-in, but the patient always decides what to use.

---

# **9\. Feature 3: Virtual Escape Worlds**

Patients can enter different immersive environments.

### **Nature**

* Beach  
* Forest  
* Garden  
* Waterfall  
* Mountains  
* Sunrise/sunset  
* Peaceful countryside

### **African/Nigerian environments**

This should be a distinctive part of the product.

Possible experiences:

* Nigerian coastal environments  
* Tropical gardens  
* African landscapes  
* Peaceful rural environments  
* Familiar cultural settings  
* African music and storytelling experiences

### **Fantasy**

* Underwater worlds  
* Space  
* Floating islands  
* Magical landscapes  
* Peaceful imaginative worlds

The purpose is not merely entertainment; each environment should have a **wellbeing purpose**.

---

# **10\. Feature 4: Relaxation & Emotional Support**

Patients should be able to select guided experiences such as:

* Breathing exercises  
* Mindfulness  
* Meditation  
* Body relaxation  
* Positive visualization  
* Guided calming sessions  
* Sleep preparation  
* Emotional grounding  
* Encouraging messages

The tone should be compassionate rather than overly clinical.

---

# **11\. Feature 5: Treatment Mode**

This should be one of the app's signature experiences.

A patient receiving chemotherapy can activate:

**“I'm in Treatment Now.”**

They then choose:

### **Take Me Somewhere**

Immersive environments.

### **Help Me Relax**

Guided calming experiences.

### **Distract Me**

Stories, music, games and engaging experiences.

### **Help Me Understand**

Simple educational information.

### **Help Me Feel Connected**

Messages and appropriate connection with loved ones.

The patient should be able to change experiences according to how they feel during treatment.

---

# **12\. Feature 6: Cancer Education**

Education should be **simple, reassuring and understandable**, rather than resembling a medical textbook.

Possible topics:

* What is cancer?  
* Understanding my treatment  
* What happens during chemotherapy?  
* Common treatment side effects  
* Medication safety  
* Nutrition and wellbeing  
* When to seek medical attention  
* Questions to ask my healthcare team  
* Understanding medicines  
* Follow-up care  
* Emotional wellbeing

### **Important principle**

The app should clearly distinguish between:

**General educational information** and **personal medical advice**.

It should encourage patients to discuss personal symptoms and treatment decisions with their healthcare professionals.

---

# **13\. Feature 7: Treatment Preparation**

Before a treatment session, patients can enter:

**“Prepare Me.”**

This experience could help them:

* Understand what to expect  
* Relax before treatment  
* Learn questions they may want to ask  
* Prepare emotionally  
* Understand common experiences associated with treatment  
* Receive encouraging messages

The objective is to replace some uncertainty with **familiarity and preparation**.

---

# **14\. Feature 8: Post-Treatment Support**

After treatment, the app can ask:

**“How are you feeling after your treatment?”**

Patients can indicate:

* Comfortable  
* Tired  
* Worried  
* Uncomfortable  
* Emotional  
* Relieved  
* Unsure

The app then offers appropriate wellbeing and educational experiences.

It should also remind the patient that concerning symptoms should be discussed with their healthcare team.

---

# **15\. Feature 9: Home Experience**

The app should remain useful outside the hospital.

The home experience can include:

* Relaxation  
* Meditation  
* Educational journeys  
* Positive storytelling  
* Sleep support  
* Gentle activities  
* Treatment preparation  
* Emotional check-ins  
* Caregiver resources

This makes the product a **cancer journey companion**, rather than something patients only use during chemotherapy.

---

# **16\. Feature 10: Caregiver Experience**

Caregivers receive their own pathway.

### **“How can I support them?”**

Content can cover:

* Understanding the patient's experience  
* Communicating with someone with cancer  
* Supporting treatment  
* Supporting emotional wellbeing  
* Helping with medicines and appointments  
* Understanding when to seek professional help  
* Looking after oneself as a caregiver

### **Shared experiences**

Where appropriate, patients could invite a caregiver into selected supportive experiences.

The patient should remain in control of what they share.

---

# **17\. Feature 11: Connection**

Patients can choose how they want to feel connected.

Potential experiences:

* Encouraging messages  
* Messages from loved ones  
* Shared calming experiences  
* Family encouragement  
* Supportive stories  
* Cancer survivor stories, where appropriately sourced and presented

The product should avoid creating unrealistic promises about cancer outcomes.

---

# **18\. Feature 12: Personal Cancer Journey**

Patients should have a visual representation of their journey.

For example:

**My Journey**

🌱 Diagnosis  
↓  
💙 Preparing  
↓  
💊 Treatment  
↓  
🌿 Recovery  
↓  
🏠 Home  
↓  
🌻 Follow-up

The journey should recognize that every patient's pathway is different.

Patients should not feel that they have failed because their journey does not follow a particular timeline.

---

# **19\. Feature 13: Personalization**

The experience should gradually learn what the patient prefers.

For example:

“You usually choose peaceful nature experiences. Would you like to visit your favourite place?”

Patients could identify:

* Favourite environments  
* Favourite sounds  
* Preferred relaxation activities  
* Preferred educational topics  
* Preferred encouragement style  
* Whether they want short or longer experiences

Personalization should always respect patient choice and privacy.

---

# **20\. Feature 14: “I Need Help”**

A highly visible option should allow a patient to indicate:

**“I need help.”**

This should not attempt to diagnose the patient.

Instead, it can guide them toward appropriate human support, such as:

* Caregiver  
* Nurse  
* Doctor  
* Pharmacist  
* Emergency services when appropriate

This is particularly important when the app is being used during treatment.

---

# **21\. Feature 15: Patient Feedback**

After an experience:

**“Did this help you?”**

Possible responses:

* Yes  
* A little  
* Not really  
* No

Then:

**“What would you like next?”**

This creates a continuous improvement loop while keeping feedback simple for tired patients.

---

# **22\. Safety & Trust Requirements**

Because the product serves people affected by cancer, trust must be central.

The app should:

* Avoid claiming to cure cancer.  
* Avoid replacing professional medical care.  
* Clearly identify educational information.  
* Encourage appropriate professional consultation.  
* Avoid giving individualized treatment decisions without appropriate clinical oversight.  
* Provide appropriate escalation when someone reports concerning symptoms.  
* Protect patient confidentiality.  
* Obtain appropriate consent for any patient information collected.  
* Avoid emotionally manipulative messaging.  
* Avoid promising particular treatment outcomes.

---

# **23\. Content Principles**

Every experience should be:

**Compassionate**  
Patients should feel respected rather than treated like a diagnosis.

**Simple**  
Information should be understandable without medical training.

**Hopeful but realistic**  
The product should support hope without making promises.

**Culturally relevant**  
African and Nigerian patients should see themselves represented.

**Inclusive**  
Different cancer types, ages, backgrounds and treatment experiences should be considered.

**Patient-controlled**  
Patients can stop, change or skip experiences at any time.

---

### **🏠 Home**

**“What do you need right now?”**

### **🌿 Explore**

All immersive experiences.

### **💙 My Journey**

Personal cancer journey and wellbeing history.

### **🤝 Caregiver**

Caregiver resources and shared experiences.

Cancer and treatment education.

### **❤️ Support**

Emotional support and connection.

---

# **25\. Differentiating Features**

The product should not compete simply by having “beautiful VR environments.”

Its differentiation should be the combination of:

### **1\. Emotion-responsive support**

The experience adapts to what the patient says they need.

### **2\. Treatment-specific experiences**

Particularly the **“I'm in Treatment Now”** mode.

### **3\. Cancer education inside immersive experiences**

Learning becomes an experience rather than just reading.

### **4\. African cultural representation**

Nigerian and African environments, stories and perspectives.

### **5\. Patient \+ caregiver ecosystem**

Both people affected by cancer receive support.

### **6\. Pharmacist-informed wellbeing**

CancerCompass can bring its pharmaceutical-care perspective into medication education and patient support.

### **7\. Journey-based support**

The experience follows the patient's changing needs from preparation through treatment and home recovery.

# **26\. Example User Story**

### **Patient: “Ada”**

Ada is about to receive chemotherapy.

She opens CancerCompass VR.

**Step 1:**  
“How are you feeling?”

She selects **Anxious**.

**Step 2:**  
“What do you need right now?”

She selects **Prepare Me**.

**Step 3:**  
The app provides a gentle treatment-preparation experience.

Afterward:

“Would you like to continue preparing, relax, or take your mind somewhere else?”

Ada chooses **Take Me Somewhere**.

She enters a peaceful Nigerian coastal environment with calming sounds.

During treatment, she activates:

**“I'm in Treatment Now.”**

She chooses **Distract Me**.

After treatment:

“How are you feeling?”

She selects **Tired**.

The app recommends a short relaxation experience and appropriate general recovery information.

This is the type of journey I would want the product to create.

---

# **27\. MVP — What We Should Build First**

Although the long-term vision is broad, I would **not launch everything at once**.

The first version should concentrate on six experiences:

1. **Emotional check-in**  
2. **“What do you need right now?”**  
3. **Treatment Mode**  
4. **Peaceful immersive environments**  
5. **Relaxation and emotional support**  
6. **Simple cancer education**

Then add caregiver experiences, connection, personalization and additional environments based on patient feedback.

This keeps the first product focused while preserving the larger vision.

---

# **28\. Success Measures**

The product should ultimately measure whether it is **helpful**, not merely whether people open it.

Key outcomes could include:

* Number of patients using the experience  
* Repeat usage  
* Completion of wellbeing experiences  
* Patient-reported reduction in anxiety/distress  
* Patient-reported usefulness during treatment  
* Patient understanding of selected educational topics  
* Patient satisfaction  
* Caregiver engagement  
* Most requested experiences  
* Experiences patients return to  
* Number of patients who report wanting to use the product again

Where possible, evaluation should distinguish **user satisfaction** from claims about clinical effectiveness.

---

# **29\. Long-Term Vision**

The long-term vision could expand CancerCompass VR from a relaxation application into a broader **immersive cancer-support ecosystem**.

Potential future areas include:

**Before diagnosis → Diagnosis → Treatment preparation → Treatment → Recovery → Survivorship → Long-term wellbeing**

It could eventually support different cancer populations, treatment types and healthcare settings while retaining the central question:

**“What do you need right now?”**

---

## **Proposed Product Positioning**

I would position it as:

**CancerCompass VR is an immersive cancer-support companion that helps patients and caregivers navigate the emotional, educational and wellbeing challenges of cancer—from preparation and treatment to recovery and life at home.**

And the core promise:

**Escape. Understand. Relax. Connect. Keep Going.**

This is a particularly strong direction for CancerCompass because it extends your existing **patient-centred pharmaceutical-care and public-health positioning** into an innovative digital health experience.

### **My strongest recommendation**

**Don't build “a VR app for cancer patients.” Build “a cancer journey companion that happens to use VR.”**

That distinction can shape the entire product, the user experience, your future partnerships with hospitals/cancer centres, and eventually your funding/grant narrative.
